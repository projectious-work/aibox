Emphasis — the second channel

Colour was the whole system. Every contrast exemption accepted above, and every user reading in greyscale, under a colour-vision deficiency, or off a bad projector, needs state carried by something other than hue. The upstream themes already declare emphasis and aibox discards it: two attributes across 61 variants.

upstream declares it

TextMate themes carry a per-scope fontStyle. Found while auditing colour:

Andromeeda inherited-class → underline
Vesper markup.bold → bold, keys → italic
Min Dark emphasis → italic, strong → bold
Laserwave none — fallback spec applies

Harvesting all 61 is a generator task: read fontStyle at build time, the way colours are read now. Hand-copying 61 style maps would rot on the first upstream bump.

degradation, not silence

An attribute the terminal cannot render substitutes another channel. It never drops the state.

italic → dim
underline → bold
strikethrough → dim
dim → (safe to drop)
bold → (never dropped)

Nine of thirteen tool surfaces take at least bold, italic and dim. Emphasis travels better than the nine-slot palette does.

Three new families — grayscale and high contrast

Ten variants, authored rather than imported, so they could be built the right way round: floor first, then role separation, then values that satisfy both. Verified by the same engine that audited the other 61.

Why these families need the second channel

A nine-slot palette assumes nine distinguishable hues. Remove hue and you have a lightness ramp with a fixed number of usable steps, and the higher the floor the fewer remain. Role separation becomes a pair: a step plus an attribute. Two roles may share a step only when their attributes differ.
